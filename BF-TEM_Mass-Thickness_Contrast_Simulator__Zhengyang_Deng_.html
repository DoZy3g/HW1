Governing Physical Equations & Soft-Matter Contrast Principles

1. Mass-Thickness Attenuation Law

In amorphous soft materials, electrons undergo incoherent elastic scattering. Those scattered beyond $\alpha_{\text{obj}}$ are intercepted by the objective aperture diaphragm, yielding exponential attenuation:

$$I(\alpha_{\text{obj}}) = I_0 \exp\!\left[-\frac{N_A \sigma_{\text{el}}(\alpha_{\text{obj}})}{A} (\rho t)\right]$$

Contrast increases exponentially with local mass-thickness $\rho t$ and effective scattering cross-section $\sigma_{\text{el}}(\alpha_{\text{obj}})$.

2. Screened Rutherford Cross-Section

Integrating the differential Rutherford cross-section from $\alpha_{\text{obj}}$ to $\pi$ shows why heavy-atom stains ($\text{OsO}_4$, $Z=76$) produce dramatic dark contrast compared to carbon/polymers ($Z \approx 6$):

$$\sigma_{\text{el}}(\alpha_{\text{obj}}) = \frac{Z^2 \lambda^4}{4\pi^3 a_0^2}\left(1+\frac{E}{E_0}\right)^2 \frac{1}{\theta_0^2 + \alpha_{\text{obj}}^2}$$

Smaller $\alpha_{\text{obj}}$ lowers the denominator $(\theta_0^2 + \alpha_{\text{obj}}^2)$, capturing a much larger fraction of scattered electrons and boosting contrast.

3. Contrast vs. Resolution Trade-Off

Choosing $\alpha_{\text{obj}}$ requires balancing contrast against two competing optical aberrations (Airy diffraction vs. spherical aberration):

$$d_{\text{tot}} \approx \sqrt{\left(\frac{0.61\lambda}{\alpha_{\text{obj}}}\right)^2 + \left(0.25 C_s \alpha_{\text{obj}}^3\right)^2}$$

An ultra-small aperture ($10\,\mu\text{m}$) blurs the $7.5\text{ nm}$ $\text{OsO}_4$ nanoparticles via diffraction; an open aperture ($>150\,\mu\text{m}$) washes out unstained polystyrene contrast and introduces $C_s \alpha^3$ blur.
